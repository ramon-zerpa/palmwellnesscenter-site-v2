## What Causes Back Pain After a Car Accident?

Most accident-related back pain comes from the sudden force that pushes the body forward and back on impact. Even crashes under 10 mph can stretch soft tissue, shift vertebrae, or irritate nerves near the spinal cord.

**Common causes include:**

- Rear-end collisions
- Seat belt and airbag force
- Side-impact twisting
- Delayed inflammation 24–72 hours after impact

Back pain after a crash isn’t always obvious on day one. Many patients think pain will go away on its own, but ignoring early symptoms is how short-term injuries turn into chronic pain management problems.

## Can Back Pain After a Car Accident Get Worse Later?

Yes. Many Tampa patients feel only “a little sore” right after an accident, then pain ramps up 24–72 hours later as inflammation builds in muscles, joints, and ligaments.

That delay is one reason people miss Florida’s 14-day PIP evaluation window.

If back pain:

- increases instead of improving
- spreads into the neck, shoulder, arm, or knee
- causes stiffness or weakness

…it may be a sign that deeper soft-tissue or disc injury is developing. Early treatment helps limit strain, protect the spine, and support safer healing.

## When To Seek Emergency Care

Go to the emergency room immediately if you experience:

- Loss of bladder or bowel control
- Severe weakness
- Inability to walk
- Intense pain with breathing
- Sudden numbness in both legs

These signs may indicate compression around the spinal cord or bone injury and require urgent help from a physician.

## Common Back Injuries After a Car Accident

Car accidents affect the back in different ways. The injuries we see most often include:

- Muscle strains and ligament sprains
- Herniated or bulging discs
- Spinal misalignments
- Facet joint irritation
- Compression fractures

In Tampa, these injuries often happen after rear-end accidents on busy corridors like Hillsborough Ave, Dale Mabry, and I-275 merges. Stop-and-go traffic and sudden braking create enough force to strain soft tissue, irritate nerves, and cause lasting pain.

Understanding which injury you have helps guide the right combination of chiropractic care, physical rehabilitation, and pain management.

## Florida PIP: Why You Need to Act Within 14 Days

Under Florida law, you must receive an evaluation within 14 days to stay eligible for Personal Injury Protection insurance. Waiting longer — even if symptoms appear late — can reduce benefits or eliminate coverage.

PIP coverage may help pay for:

- physician visits
- chiropractic treatment
- physical therapy
- diagnostic imaging
- pain management

If you were in a car accident, don’t wait for pain to get worse. Get checked as soon as possible.
